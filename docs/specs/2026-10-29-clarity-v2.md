# Clarity v2: specific intent and divergence-driven decisions

Approved in the current conversation on 2026-10-29: canary thresholds, scenario
approval replacing prose playback approval, North Star questioning until every
rubric passes (no cap), proposing an extrapolated answer for the user to confirm whenever an answer is vague, vague
words prompting for higher clarity without blocking, and the divergence-first approach. Extends the
[clarity v2 proposal](../plans/2026-10-29-clarity-v2-proposal.md), whose claims,
quote tiling, novelty tripwire, scenarios and canary review still apply.

## 1. Goal

Intent keeps asking until each part of the user's intent is clear by measured
criteria, not by the model's opinion. Then it decides every build-changing choice,
or explicitly delegates it, using observed divergence between candidate builds.

"Clear enough" has no single proven number in the literature. This spec combines
three kinds of evidence and keeps each threshold configurable:

| Evidence | Source | Used as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form rules for well-written requirements | INCOSE GtWR v4 rules R6–R35; ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 characteristics | Deterministic lexicon and structure checks |
| Detector reliability | Femmer et al. 2017: precision 0.96 subjective language, 0.81 vague adverbs/adjectives, 0.26 pronouns, 0.33 negation | Which flags prompt automatically and which need a judge first |
| Agreement between readers | Krippendorff: α ≥ 0.800 customary, 0.667 lowest acceptable limit | Whether independent readers derive the same meaning |
| Divergence between builds | ClarifyGPT (Mu et al. 2023): ask only when sampled solutions disagree; GPT-4 Pass@1 70.96% → 80.80% | How decisions are discovered |
| Question value | Kobalczyk et al. 2025 (expected information gain); SAGE-Agent 2025 (stop when net gain < α·confidence) | Which decision to ask next and when to stop asking decisions |
| Reversibility | Bezos 2015/2016 letters (one-way/two-way doors, ~70% information); Poppendieck (last responsible moment) | Ask vs delegate vs defer |
| Decision quality | Spetzler et al., *Decision Quality*: a decision is no better than its weakest of six links | Per-decision completeness score |
| Answer fatigue | Galesic & Bosnjak 2009: later answers are faster, shorter and more uniform | Why Intent proposes drafts instead of demanding more detail; fatigue is recorded for evaluation |

## 2. North Star fields and their rubrics

The six fields from the proposal (§3.1). Each field passes only when **all** of
its checks pass. There is no question cap.

### 2.1 Checks applied to every field

- **G1 Lexicon prompts (deterministic, non-blocking):** flags vague quantifiers or
  adjectives (INCOSE R7), escape clauses such as "if necessary" or "as appropriate"
  (R8), open endings such as "etc." or "and so on" (R9), absolutes such as "always"
  or "every" (R26), and subjective language ("easy", "nice", "user-friendly"). A
  flag never fails the field by itself. Each flagged word is prompted **once** for
  higher clarity, quoting it and offering 2–3 sharper options plus "keep my
  wording". G1 is satisfied when every flag has been prompted and the user either
  sharpened the word or kept it; kept wording is recorded as `kept by user`.
  Lists live in a new `backend/app/clarity/lexicon.py`, versioned and tested.
- **G2 Flag handling by precision:** subjective language and vague adjectives are
  prompted automatically. Low-precision signals (pronouns, negations, comparatives)
  go to a judge call first, which decides with a recorded reason whether to prompt.
  This keeps unreliable detectors from producing pointless questions.
- Kept vague wording still has to pass the field rubric, G3 and G4. If two readers
  cannot agree on what "fast" means, G4 fails and asks about that probe; the word
  itself was not the failure.
- **G3 Read-back:** Intent restates the field in one sentence, and the user confirms
  it. A correction is a new utterance and reruns the checks.
- **G4 Reader agreement:** two isolated Nano readers, given only the field text,
  answer the same 6–8 multiple-choice probes about it (for example, "Must it work on
  a phone? yes / no / cannot tell"). The app computes nominal Krippendorff's α.
  - α ≥ 0.80 and no "cannot tell" answer: pass.
  - 0.667 ≤ α < 0.80: ask one targeted follow-up about the disagreeing probe.
  - α < 0.667, or any "cannot tell": the field fails, and the probe becomes the follow-up.

  α over 6–8 items is noisy, so any disagreement on a probe tagged one-way also fails,
  whatever the α.

### 2.2 Field-specific rubrics

| Field | Checks (all required) | Weak → passing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who & when** | W1: names a person or group the user can identify, not "users" or "people". W2: an observable trigger situation (job-story "When…"). W3: device or place. W4: frequency. | "people who read" → "me, on my laptop, several times a day when I find an article I can't read now" |
| **Job** | J1: one verb and one object (INCOSE R19, no and/or combinators). J2: names no feature or technology (R31). | "manage articles and stuff" → "save an article to read later" |
| **Today** | T1: names the current workaround. T2: names one concrete pain with it. | "it's messy" → "I leave tabs open and lose them when Chrome restarts" |
| **Good looks like** | O1: direction, measure, object and context (ODI outcome statement). O2 (prompt once, non-blocking like G1): a number with a unit (R34, R6); offer 2–3 numbers plus "keep my wording". O3: no feature named. | "fast and easy" → "find a saved article in under 10 seconds" |
| **Fails if** | F1: an If/then failure the user could observe (EARS unwanted-behaviour pattern). F2: at least one, specific to this product. | "if it's buggy" → "if a saved link disappears after reload" |
| **Ambition** | A1: one of throwaway / personal tool / shared with a few people / product. A2: who else uses it. A3: expected lifespan. | "just an app" → "personal tool, only me, used for months" |

Structural checks (W2, J1, O1, F1 and others) are judged by a single batched Nano call,
because they need parsing. Its verdict must name the failing rule ID. A verdict
without a rule ID counts as a failure of the judge, not of the user's field.

### 2.3 The questioning loop

```text
field text -> G1 lexicon -> field rubric -> G4 readers -> G3 read-back
                 |               |              |              |
          flag (prompt once,   fail           fail          correct
          keep allowed)          |              |              |
                 +-------- follow-up (one rule, 2-3 options) <-+
                                         |
                                       pass -> next field
```

- Each follow-up targets **one** failing rule, quotes the user's words, and offers
  2–3 concrete options plus "other". The IN3 results (about one detail per round,
  about 2.7 options) support this format.
  Example: *You said "fast". Which is closest: under 2 seconds, under 10 seconds,
  or within a minute?*
- **Order:** Who & when, Job, Ambition first, because they set proportionality for
  the rest. Then Today, Good looks like, Fails if.
- **No cap:** the loop continues until every check passes. The user may pause at
  any time; the gate stays closed and progress is saved.

### 2.4 Propose and confirm (approved 2026-10-29)

Someone with an idea rarely knows the details a builder needs, so Intent, as the
builder, fills them in and then checks with the user. Every North Star follow-up,
for a missing, vague, failing or ambiguous answer, is a proposal:

- Intent extrapolates the most plausible specific answer from everything the user
  said. It fills each gap with a concrete, proportionate assumption and lists
  those assumptions. The question reads: *My best guess from what you told me:
  "…". I filled in: … Does that fit?*
- It offers 2–3 alternative drafts, each screened for vague words (G1), plus
  "Keep my wording" for vague-word and measurable-outcome prompts. The user can
  pick a draft, edit one before sending, or write their own answer.
- A picked draft is recorded as the user's turn with the exact draft text, so the
  exact-quote rule holds. It is labeled as drafted by Intent in the transcript.
  A field written entirely from picked drafts needs no separate read-back (G3),
  because the user has just confirmed that exact text. The rubric and G4 still run.
- Intent never adopts a draft without the user's selection or edit.
- Fatigue signals (Galesic & Bosnjak: answer length halves against the first three
  answers, two consecutive "I don't know"/"you decide", repeated one-word replies)
  are recorded for evaluation. Drafting does not wait for fatigue.

The same principle applies after the North Star. Implementation proposals (claims
labeled proposal) and "Choices I made for you" are shown as **What I filled in for
you** beside the scenarios. Approval covers them, and a veto or correction reopens them.

## 3. Decision framework (divergence-first)

### 3.1 Discover by divergence

After the North Star passes, Intent samples **N = 4** candidate specs (Nano, high
temperature) from the North Star and ledger. It then asks each candidate the
proposal's prediction probes (§7.2) plus probes generated from the six critic lenses.
Each probe where the candidates disagree becomes a decision, with the clusters
of answers as its options. Critic candidates remain as an extra source. A critic
question on which all candidates agree is dropped (ClarifyGPT's rule).

### 3.2 Choose the next question

Score each open decision:

`score = split × importance × reversibility_weight − repeat_penalty`

- **split:** normalized entropy of how candidates divide across the options. An
  even split is the most informative (Kobalczyk et al.).
- **importance:** Kano class. Must-be = 3, performance = 2, delighter = 1.
- **reversibility_weight:** one-way = 3, two-way = 1.
- **repeat_penalty:** λ × earlier questions on the same aspect (SAGE-Agent).

Ask the top decision, one per turn, with 2–3 options.

### 3.3 Disposition

Rows apply in order; the first match wins.

| Decision type | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| One-way door (data model, identity, external integration, deleting or overwriting user data) | Always asked. Cannot be delegated. |
| Two-way door where ≥ 3 of 4 candidates agree (≥ 70%, per Bezos) | Default to the majority option; listed in "Choices I made for you" with a one-click veto. |
| Must-be, two-way, fewer than 3 of 4 agree | Asked. |
| Delighter (Kano) | Default or exclude, stated in the North Star's terms. |
| Not needed until a later phase (last responsible moment) | Deferred with a `due` phase; the phase planner must resolve it before that phase starts. |

**Stop rule for decisions:** stop asking when the best remaining score is below the
configurable `DECISION_STOP_THRESHOLD`, following SAGE-Agent. Remaining two-way
decisions become defaults. One-way decisions are never stopped. This rule applies to
decisions only; North Star fields follow §2.3.

### 3.4 Decision record (MADR fields)

```text
Decision D3
  status        open | decided | delegated | deferred(due=P2) | excluded
  context       one sentence, cites ledger/claim IDs
  drivers       North Star fields it serves, e.g. good-looks-like, fails-if
  options       2-3, each with code difference, pros, cons
  outcome       chosen option, "because" + a driver
  owner         user (answer quote) | delegated (majority, veto offered)
  consequences  claims it adds or removes
  confirmation  scenario ID that shows the build follows it
  reversibility one-way | two-way      kano  must-be | performance | delighter
```

### 3.5 Decision quality score

Each decision is scored on the six Decision Quality links. The app computes each
link from the record:

| Link | Present when |
| --- | --- |
| Frame | context cites claims; drivers name a North Star field |
| Alternatives | ≥ 2 options with distinct code differences |
| Information | options grounded in candidate divergence or cited ledger text |
| Values | outcome's "because" names a driver |
| Reasoning | consequences list at least the claims added or removed |
| Commitment | user answer quote or delegation record, and a confirmation scenario |

Quality is the **minimum** link. One-way decisions need every link present. Two-way
decisions need every link except an explicit user answer.

### 3.6 Wasted-question metric

A question whose answer did not change any candidate cluster, claim or scenario is
counted as wasted, as in CLAM's penalty. The wasted share appears in eval reports and
is used to tune `DECISION_STOP_THRESHOLD`. It does not affect the gate.

## 4. Gate additions

The proposal's gate (§8) gains:

1. Every North Star field passes its field rubric, G3 and G4, and every G1 flag has
   been prompted once (sharpened or kept by user).
2. Every one-way decision is user-owned with full Decision Quality.
3. No open decision scores at or above the stop threshold.
4. Every deferred decision has a `due` phase that exists in the plan contract.
5. The canary review meets 100% of planted cost-2/3 defects and ≥ 80% overall.
6. The user approves the scenarios and "What I filled in for you" (proposals and
   choices made for them). This approval replaces prose playback approval.

## 5. Budget and limits

New Nano calls: two readers and one rubric judge per field attempt; four candidate
specs and their probe answers once per analysis. All share `SESSION_BUDGET_USD`
and reserve before dispatch. Budget exhaustion keeps the gate closed and says so.
It never passes a field. No live cost figure is claimed until measured.
